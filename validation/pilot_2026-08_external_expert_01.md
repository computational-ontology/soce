# 1 · Design

First external expert pilot of the deposited instrument, run in the participant's
own AI assistant (ChatGPT) as a portable skill — no SOCE infrastructure, no
dedicated compute. Participant: **Nirmali Wijegoonawardena** (University of Colombo,
Sri Lanka), specialist in International Relations and peace and conflict
studies — named here with her written authorisation (28 August 2026).
Total time invested: ~3 hours across two cases.

- **Case A (assigned, usability check)** — Apartheid South Africa, 1948–1994.
  Appendix B contains the anchor signature for this case, so Case A tests
  whether the instrument is *usable*, not a blind reliability trial.
- **Case B (participant's own field)** — the Rohingya in Myanmar, 1982–2017,
  coded from UN FFM, Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch documentation
  supplied by the participant.

Ideology labels were not coded (blinded under §0.7). Every value below is the
AI-assisted proposal **as human-validated by the expert**; all flags are
preserved verbatim from the coding sheets — errors are data.

# 3 · Flags preserved from the coding sheets

Case A: (1)–(3) the three anchor divergences above (V: D vs P possibly a §5.5
rubric ambiguity); (4) N3.vel unresolved — no temporal boundary satisfies the
two-source threshold; (5) N3.camo unresolved — ideological legitimation better
evidenced than aesthetic camouflage; (6)–(7) see §4-a. A named brake was
recorded on IV→ᵐV (economic dependence on Black labour, political resistance
and institutional constraints limiting translation of the obedience machinery
into differentiated treatment). Genocide predictor not activated (N2.invcaus =
NO). Case B: N3.vel flagged uncertain; two produced Others recorded (Rohingya
principal; Muslims more broadly, secondary).

# 4 · Issues opened for codebook v0.2

a. **E-layer schema vs Appendix B (usability defect).** Appendix B identifies
   II→IV as Apartheid's dominant link, but II→IV is not among the eleven links
   permitted by §1.7. The expert's judgement, preserved here: this cannot be
   reproduced from the coding scheme as written and must not be silently
   mapped onto another link. The τ₂ precedent for Case A therefore remains
   flagged, not reconciled.
b. **D/E intensity thresholds.** The longest hesitations concentrated on
   adjacent-intensity distinctions (D vs E) whose resolution depends on strict
   evidentiary thresholds; rubric guidance to be sharpened.
c. **Validation workflow length.** The pilot took ~3 h against a ~90-minute
   target. v0.2 goal: the assistant completes the mechanical coding and
   presents only the essential points requiring expert judgement.
d. **Constructs proposed for consideration** (from peace and conflict
   research): forms and escalation of violence, territorial control,
   displacement, resistance, and mediation/peacebuilding mechanisms — relevant
   to how exclusion develops into conflict and how it may be constrained.

# 5 · Assessment summary (paraphrased)

Rubrics for node intensities and the Todorov axes were generally answerable
from the supplied sources. The instrument's refusal to produce an aggregate
exclusion score was judged both a strength (complex exclusion processes should
not be reduced to a potentially misleading single number) and a limitation
(early-warning practice sometimes needs a simple indicator for rapid
comparison). The resulting structural analyses were judged useful — Case B's
signature capturing the structural core of the case while underrepresenting
phase dynamics of displacement and the changing political-military context —
and the participant would use the instrument again for comparative
peace-and-conflict research on exclusion, identity construction and
institutionalized discrimination, conditional on the workflow streamlining and
rubric clarifications recorded above.
